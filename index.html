EDITORIAL Securing the Northeast

EDITORIAL

Securing the Northeast: Indian Army’s Role in Stability, Peace & National Security

The Northeast is a region of remarkable diversity, strategic importance, rich culture and immense potential. Its unique geography, international borders and varied communities make it an important part of India’s national security and development landscape.

This e-magazine, “Securing the Northeast: Indian Army’s Role in Stability, Peace & National Security,” is my attempt to explore the region beyond the conventional headlines of conflict and security challenges. Through recent developments from across the Northeast, I have tried to present a broader picture of how security, peace, development and society are closely connected.

The role of the Indian Army in the Northeast extends beyond its primary responsibility of safeguarding the nation's borders. Its engagement with local communities, support during emergencies, youth-oriented initiatives and contribution to confidence-building demonstrate the wider relationship between security forces and the people they serve.

At the same time, lasting peace cannot be achieved through security measures alone. Development, education, employment, infrastructure, sports and opportunities for young people are equally important in building a stable and prosperous region. The Northeast has repeatedly demonstrated its potential through its talented athletes, students, entrepreneurs, artists and communities.

Keeping this broader perspective in mind, this magazine brings together recent developments under five major themes: Sports & Achievements, Regional News, Development, Security & Strategic Affairs, and Society & Youth. The articles have been selected from recent news published from September 2026 onwards, with an emphasis on developments that contribute to understanding the changing security and social landscape of the region.

As a student, researching and compiling these stories has also helped me understand that national security is not simply about borders or military strength. It is also about peace within communities, trust between people and institutions, economic opportunities, connectivity, resilience and the aspirations of the younger generation.

The Northeast is not merely India's frontier; it is an integral part of India's present and future. Its progress and stability have significance far beyond the region itself.

Through this e-magazine, I hope to highlight both the challenges and the achievements of the Northeast while recognising the many individuals, communities and institutions contributing to its journey towards greater peace, security and development.

I hope this magazine provides readers with a meaningful and balanced perspective on the Northeast and encourages a deeper appreciation of its people, its strategic importance and its growing role in India's national story.

— Student Editor

Army destroys three suspected IEDs in Manipur’s Jiribam

5 September 2026 | The Times of India | Manipur
Photo: The Times of India
Indian Army personnel engaged in neutralizing the suspected improvised explosive devices found in Jiribam district.

In a significant operation aimed at maintaining peace and preventing violence, the Indian Army successfully located and destroyed three suspected Improvised Explosive Devices (IEDs) in the restive Jiribam district of Manipur. The coordinated operation was launched based on specific intelligence inputs regarding the movement and planting of explosives by insurgent groups seeking to destabilize the region.

The swift action by the bomb disposal squads of the Army ensured that the devices were neutralized without any collateral damage or loss of civilian life. Security forces have heightened their vigilance across the district following the incident, intensifying area domination patrols and continuing search operations to apprehend those responsible for planting the IEDs.

The swift neutralization of these explosives highlights the constant vigilance of our forces in ensuring the safety of civilians in conflict-prone areas.

Centre extends AFSPA in parts of Manipur, Arunachal and Nagaland for six months

25 September 2026 | The Indian Express | Multiple States
Photo: The Indian Express
The continued application of AFSPA aims to provide forces with the necessary legal framework to conduct counter-insurgency operations.

The Ministry of Home Affairs (MHA) has officially announced the extension of the Armed Forces (Special Powers) Act, 1958, in specific "disturbed areas" across Manipur, Nagaland, and Arunachal Pradesh for a further period of six months. The notification underscores the Centre's assessment that the security situation in these pockets, characterized by volatile ethnic dynamics and sporadic militant activities, necessitates the continued presence and specialized operational powers of the armed forces.

While AFSPA has been systematically rolled back from numerous districts across the Northeast over the past few years owing to an overall improvement in the security climate, areas deemed sensitive-particularly those sharing porous international borders or experiencing internal displacement and unrest-remain under its purview. Security agencies argue that the Act is vital for maintaining public order and neutralizing armed threats effectively.
